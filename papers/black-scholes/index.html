Reading guide to the original Black–Scholes paper

The Pricing of Options and Corporate Liabilities

Fischer Black and Myron Scholes, Journal of Political Economy 81(3), 1973, pp. 637–654. Publisher record and DOI · University-hosted reading copy · Return to the paper library.

The central mechanism is replication. Under the paper’s idealized assumptions, a continuously adjusted stock position and a cash account can reproduce a European option. No arbitrage then ties the option’s value to the cost of that strategy. The stock’s expected return disappears from the pricing equation; its volatility remains an essential input. The result depends on the model, rather than on a claim that all real-world options have one observable market price.

Read the market assumptions on printed page 640, then the hedge and differential equation on pages 641–643. The closed-form call formula appears on page 644, with the hedge ratio on page 645. The formula uses a stock paying no dividends, constant volatility, a constant risk-free rate, continuous stock paths, frictionless trading and the ability to borrow and sell short. European put–call parity appears on page 647.

The second half connects corporate liabilities to options. For a firm with one zero-coupon debt issue, equity pays the assets remaining after debt is repaid. That is the payoff of a call on firm assets, with debt face value as the strike. Debt receives the smaller of firm assets and its face value. Read pages 649–650, then the qualifications for more complicated liabilities on pages 651–652.

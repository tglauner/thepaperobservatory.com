# Rank ideas by what a person can learn

Apply after inspecting the source. Scores express editorial judgment, not a measured probability of success. Keep citation rank beside the score without feeding it into the score.

## Scorecard

Rate each component 0–5. Compute `sum(weight × rating / 5)` for a total out of 100 and explain the reasons.

| Component | Weight | A strong candidate offers |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Explainable mechanism | 25 | A small model/comparison explains why an outcome changes; assumptions can be stated honestly. |
| Meaningful interaction | 20 | A visitor changes a consequential variable, chooses a hypothesis or diagnoses a failure and sees a coherent response. |
| Misconception and transfer | 20 | A plausible initial belief is challenged, and a new case tests whether the distinction was learned. |
| Source support and fidelity | 15 | Source results/figures/equations support the lesson, including qualifications and boundary cases. |
| Reproduction feasibility | 10 | A deterministic reconstruction fits the static site using accessible data or clearly labeled synthetic inputs. |
| Collection value | 10 | A distinct perspective reaches Foundation/Advanced readers and complements existing exhibits. |

Anchors: **0** absent/unworkable; **1** weak; **2** promising with substantial gaps; **3** useful with manageable compromises; **4** strong and clearly supported; **5** exceptional and demonstrable. Apply the full scale. Moving a slider must change an idea the visitor can explain.

Keep **evidence confidence** (`high`, `medium`, `low`) and **build readiness** (`ready_for_design`, `needs_reproduction_work`, `needs_full_text`, `not_suitable`) separate. Inaccessibility limits evidence, not necessarily scientific quality. Abstract-only scores are provisional and cannot be `ready_for_design`. An appealing unsupported claim needs a lower source-fidelity score and a reading/reproduction action.

Use whole-point totals. For close scores prefer the clearer lesson or more varied collection contribution and explain the judgment. State alternative weights when they better match the user's priorities.

## A learning brief

Connect the visitor experience with enough specificity for the companion skill to implement it:

1. **Question and misconception:** One answerable question, wrong-but-reasonable prediction and learning outcome the visitor can demonstrate.
2. **Prediction:** Commit to a forecast/ranking/diagnosis before revealing the result; do not depend on hidden assumptions.
3. **Manipulation:** Controls, units, ranges, starting state and reset. Explain why they test the idea. A few consequential controls usually beat an unstructured panel.
4. **Consequence:** The responsive diagram/comparison/quantity. Preserve scale and uncertainty; do not animate fabricated empirical findings.
5. **Explanation:** Connect visible change to mechanism with pages and Foundation language. Advanced may expose derivation, identification/evaluation design and counterexamples.
6. **Transfer:** A genuinely new case with feedback about reasoning.
7. **Boundary:** When the lesson fails or is ambiguous. Surface leakage, costs, nonstationarity, omitted variables, misspecification or causal limits where relevant.
8. **Implementation/evidence:** Numerical primitives, data rights/size, a limiting-case check, reading status and source anchors. Label paper findings, reconstructions and teaching extensions.

## Generate alternatives faithfully

Sketch alternative ways to reveal the same mechanism for leading papers, then select the clearest. These are creative prompts, not preset exhibit templates:

- **Information time machine:** Reveal what was knowable at each decision; move a feature across the cutoff and expose leakage. A synthetic case teaches timing without reproducing claimed historical returns.
- **Market ecology:** Vary the number/diversity of learning agents and see feedback/coordination. State whether the source uses simulated or observed markets.
- **Evidence courtroom:** Compare a headline, prediction and data; ask which inference is justified. Counterfactual labels must reflect the identification strategy.
- **Representation microscope:** Probe/rotate a representation and track information gained/lost. A 2D teaching projection cannot validate full-dimensional performance.
- **Human–model relay:** Allocate decisions between people/models and explore complementarity or correlated errors. Separate illustrative distributions from source estimates.
- **Uncertainty weather map:** Vary shift/calibration/thresholds and show statistical accuracy alongside economic consequences; separate history, model uncertainty and synthetic outcomes.

A paper's actual structure determines its visual. Avoid spectacle without mechanism, treating clicks as evidence of learning, or giving a causal slider to an observational association.

## Portfolio judgment

Inspect the collection as a sequence: information/leakage, representation, decisions under uncertainty, market interaction, human judgment and institutions. A survey may make a useful map but a narrower reconstructable mechanism may make a better first experiment. Put older/out-of-source discoveries in a separate extension queue, outside the requested universe.

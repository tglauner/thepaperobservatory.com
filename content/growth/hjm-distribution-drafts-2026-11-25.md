## Main demonstration

**Opening:** What if an interest-rate model started with the entire forward curve?

**Narration:** The Heath–Jarrow–Morton framework does just that. Choose an initial forward curve and specify how its rates can move. Under the risk-neutral measure and the model's assumptions, the no-arbitrage condition then links the forward-rate drift to those volatility choices. In this interactive teaching model, change the curve and compare one- and two-factor responses to a synthetic shock. These curves are illustrations, not market estimates or forecasts. No-arbitrage does not choose the volatility model for you.

## Short derivative 1 — the drift constraint

In an HJM model, you cannot independently pick every forward-rate drift after choosing volatility and imposing the model's risk-neutral no-arbitrage condition. The condition constrains drift given the volatility assumptions; it does not make those assumptions true. ## Short derivative 2 — two kinds of expectation

A risk-neutral drift is used for valuation under a pricing measure. It should not be read as a forecast of real-world future rates: the physical-measure dynamics also involve market prices of risk.
